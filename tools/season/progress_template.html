How the numbers are made

Source
One coach report per song (Claude Opus 5 through the local Claude Code CLI): a survey pass at about 3 frames a second, then eight slow-motion looks at 10 fps. Tempo and phrase changes come from the audio, not the model.
Phrase changes
Boundaries come from the music itself: beat-synced harmony and texture features, a novelty curve, energy changes, and the 8-count grid, with a preference for 32- and 16-count sections. A strict judging pass then reviews a short frame burst at each boundary and credits only a deliberate musical choice (a hit, a stop, a level change, a release, a styling accent, a sharp change of direction or speed). Two decoy windows per song, count 1 of an 8 that is not a phrase change, measure the judge's false-positive rate; the chance level shown under the phrase chart is that rate. Read the hit rate against it. Songs the strict pass has not reached yet are marked as such in Song by song.
Late or behind the beat
Mentions of late, behind, rushing, or catching up in the slow-motion count notes and the survey notes. On-beat mentions are counted the same way. Rough, but the same rule for every song.
Stalled seconds
Total length of refine notes about closed position, hugging, or standing in place.
Dead time at the top
Length of the earliest note that complains about standing or not yet dancing. Zero when nothing was flagged.
Theme families
Keyword matches on the refine notes; a note can land in more than one family. Treat differences under about one note per song as noise.
Exclusions
Songs where the model could not confirm the couple (focus under 50%) or never saw the opening are kept in the table but left out of every mean.
